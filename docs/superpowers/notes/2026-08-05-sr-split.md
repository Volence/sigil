## §1 — THE SPELLING, AND ITS DEFENCE

`sr.mask` / `sr.ccr`, as the ledger row presumed. `preserves(ints)` was the
floated alternative and is REJECTED on one argument: the split names the
PARTITION OF THE MACHINE REGISTER, not a synonym for one use of it. `ints`
invents a new noun for the system byte, unsearchable against the `sr` the
instructions spell; `sr.mask` and `sr.ccr` read as what they are — the two
halves of the thing `move.w sr, -(sp)` moves — and the pair is self-evidently
a partition (`sr = sr.mask ∪ sr.ccr`), which is the fact every check in this
parcel computes with. `sr.mask` names the system byte (the interrupt mask,
with the trace/supervisor bits it shares the byte with — stated in the
`SrCover` doc, not hidden). Bare `ccr` stays a non-token and now STEERS to
`sr.ccr` — one spelling per half, no synonyms.

**Bare `sr` remains legal and means both halves.** No adopter moves; six
bare-`sr` procs remain and all six honestly round-trip.

**Redundant co-declaration FOLDS.** `preserves(sr, sr.ccr)` covers what
`preserves(sr)` covers, with no diagnostic — a reglist denotes a SET UNION
everywhere in the grammar (`d0, d0-d3` folds silently today), and making the
SR family the one place union is an error would be a second rule for the same
grammar position. The line the house rule actually draws is kept: union
WITHIN a clause is well-defined and folds; contradiction ACROSS clauses is
diagnosed, never resolved — now per half, so `preserves(sr)` against
`clobbers(sr.ccr)` errors while `preserves(sr.mask)` beside it is the legal
honest partition.

Grammar: reglist endpoints accept one dotted suffix (`parser.rs
reg_segment_name`), validity deferred to lowering per the clause grammar's
standing rule — `clobbers(d0.mask)` is a lowering-time `[proc.clobber-invalid]`,
and the old parser refuses the dot outright (which is half of §5).
